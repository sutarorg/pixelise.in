Amazon is the most prescriptive marketplace most sellers will ever list on. Its image requirements are not suggestions: a main image that fails review can have a listing suppressed, a variation rejected, or an entire ASIN hidden from search until it is fixed.

The complication is that Amazon's rules live behind Seller Central, change by category, and are written for compliance rather than clarity. This guide explains how the requirements work, what reviewers look for, and how to check the current rule instead of trusting a blog post — including this one.

## Why Amazon images are different

On your own storefront, an unusual product photo is a design decision. On Amazon, it is a compliance question, because the image sits in a standardised results page where shoppers compare near-identical tiles.

Three consequences follow:

1. **The main image is heavily constrained** so that tiles remain comparable. Deviating makes your listing look like an ad among products.
2. **Secondary images carry the selling.** Because the main image is regulated, the persuasion happens in images two through seven.
3. **Category matters.** Grocery, apparel, jewellery, and consumables have additional expectations around what may be shown and how.

## The main image: what to verify in Seller Central

Amazon's guidance has long required the main image to show the product on a pure white background, filling most of the frame, with no added text, logos, watermarks, borders or props. Many categories have historically required the product to be a real photograph of the item rather than an illustration.

Rather than quoting numbers that may have changed, verify these five things in your own Seller Central help page for your category before you upload:

- **Background:** pure white, with no visible shadow gradient behind the product.
- **Framing:** the product occupies the majority of the frame, with even margins.
- **Purity:** no text overlays, badges, borders, props, or additional items not included in the sale.
- **Content:** only the product and what the customer receives.
- **Format:** accepted file types, minimum dimensions for zoom, and file size limits.

If your category has additional rules — apparel on a model, jewellery scale, food preparation suggestions — check the category-specific style guide rather than the general one.

| Image slot | Job | Typical content |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Main | Identify the product | Clean packshot, pure white, product fills frame |
| 2 | Second angle or the back | Shows construction, ports, closure |
| 3 | Scale | Product in hand or beside a familiar reference |
| 4 | Detail | Texture, stitching, ingredients panel, controls |
| 5 | In use | Lifestyle scene that matches the actual use |
| 6 | What is in the box | Contents laid out, including accessories |
| 7 | Comparison or compatibility | Size chart, variant comparison, fit guidance |

That is a structure, not a rule. Amazon allows more images than most sellers use; the ones that earn their slot answer questions that reduce returns.

## Preparing files that survive review

- **Cut out on pure white.** Not off-white, not a light grey studio sweep. Check the values in an image editor: the background should read as white, without a gradient.
- **Centre and scale consistently.** The product should occupy the same rough proportion of the frame across a variation family, so the tiles look like a set.
- **Export at the largest size your category allows** so zoom works. A small image that the marketplace upscales looks soft next to competitors.
- **Use sRGB.** Wide-gamut files can shift colour after processing and make a brand colour look wrong on the detail page.
- **Name files logically** — brand, product, variation, slot. It helps when you are auditing thirty ASINs.
- **Keep the master file.** Marketplaces reprocess uploads; keep the original for future corrections.

## When a listing gets suppressed

Image-related suppression usually happens for a handful of reasons, and the fix is usually faster than the panic suggests:

1. **Main image is not on pure white.** The most common cause. Re-cut and resubmit.
2. **Text or graphics added to the main image.** Badges such as "Sale" or "New" violate main image rules even if they are true.
3. **Product does not fill enough of the frame**, or is cropped at an edge.
4. **Image resolution too low** for the category's zoom requirement.
5. **Product mismatch** — the image shows a different variant, bundle, or size than the ASIN.
6. **Watermark or seller logo** in a secondary image, which some categories disallow.

When you fix one of these, resubmit the image and allow time for re-review. If the suppression persists, open a case referencing the specific policy rather than re-uploading repeatedly.

## Variations, bundles and family consistency

Amazon groups variations — size, colour, pack count — under one parent listing, and the image set carries most of the differentiation. Three rules keep a family coherent:

- **Same angle, same framing, same background** across every child. A shopper swiping between colours should see the product change, not the photography.
- **One image per variation**, showing the actual variant. Reusing the parent image for a colour that differs is a common cause of returns and complaints.
- **A clear bundle image** when the listing sells a multipack or a set: lay the contents out so the quantity is obvious at thumbnail size.

For bundles, the main image rules still apply — pure white, product only — but the product is now the whole bundle. Photograph everything included, in the arrangement it ships in, and add a "what is in the box" secondary image. If an accessory is not included, it must not appear in a way that suggests it is.

Family consistency is also a merchandising advantage: a coherent set of tiles reads as a professional brand in a results page full of mismatched photography.

## How AI-generated imagery fits Amazon

This is the category where generated imagery needs the most restraint, for a structural reason: Amazon's main image is a compliance artefact, and generated changes to the product itself are exactly what review is designed to catch.

Reasonable uses:

- **Background cleanup** on a photo you actually shot — removing a stray reflection or unifying the white.
- **Concepting secondary images** before you brief a photographer.
- **Drafting lifestyle scenes** for categories where a contextual shot is allowed.

Unreasonable uses:

- Generating the product itself for the main image.
- Altering logos, nutrition panels, ingredient lists or certifications.
- Creating "in use" scenes that imply results the product cannot deliver.
- Adding text or badges with a generative tool and assuming they will pass review.

If you generate anything, keep the source photograph and be able to demonstrate that the product shown matches what ships.

## A pre-upload checklist

- [ ] Main image on pure white, product centred, no props or text.
- [ ] Product fills the frame with even margins.
- [ ] Category-specific rules checked in Seller Central for this ASIN.
- [ ] Minimum resolution confirmed for zoom.
- [ ] Secondary images cover: back, scale, detail, in use, contents.
- [ ] Colour checked against the physical product or a calibrated reference.
- [ ] No watermarks, borders or added badges.
- [ ] Variation family uses a consistent angle and scale.
- [ ] Files named logically and kept in an archive.
- [ ] Image order reviewed on mobile, where only the first few are visible.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does Amazon require a pure white background?

Amazon's main image guidance has long required a pure white background for most categories, with no visible shadow behind the product. Requirements can differ by category, so confirm the current rule in Seller Central for the category you are listing in.

### Can I use the same images on Amazon and Shopify?

Usually yes, except the main image: Amazon's main image is stricter about purity and framing than most storefronts expect. Secondary images can often be shared, but crop ratios differ, so check how the marketplace displays them.

### Do Amazon images need to be a certain size?

Amazon has historically set a minimum size for zoom and a maximum file size, with category variation. Check the current specification for your category before exporting; uploading a large master and letting the marketplace resize is usually safer than uploading a small file.

### Why was my listing suppressed after uploading a new image?

The most common causes are a main image that is not on pure white, added text or graphics, insufficient resolution, or a product mismatch with the ASIN. Fix the specific issue, resubmit, and allow time for re-review.
